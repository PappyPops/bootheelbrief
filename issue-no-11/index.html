The Lead · Mistletoe & Moss opens at noon today

The doors open at noon today, and if you've been meaning to get a head start on Christmas, this is the morning to do it. Mistletoe & Moss — the Junior League of Lake Charles' holiday market — takes over the Lake Charles Event Center starting today, with more than 90 vendors, one-of-a-kind gifts, holiday décor, clothing, art, and Santa on site for the kids.

It's a three-day affair: today noon to 9, Saturday 9 to 6, Sunday 10 to 3. Tonight's the night to go if you like your shopping with a little sparkle — Sip & Shop runs 6 to 9 p.m. with festive music, food from local vendors, and signature cocktails. Kids 8 and under get in free, and Santa's Workshop runs Saturday and Sunday with holiday crafts, cookie decorating, and photos with Santa and Mrs. Claus.

The Junior League has run this market for years now, and it's become the unofficial start of the holidays around here — the weekend the Event Center fills up and everybody starts comparing lists. Go early if you can. The good stuff goes fast. Market details →

Business Spotlight · The Imperial Calcasieu Museum

Keys and sheet music — a nod to Lake Charles jazz legend Nellie Lutcher, whose exhibit is on now.

Tucked under the 375-year-old Sallier Oak at 204 W. Sallier St., the Imperial Calcasieu Museum has been telling Southwest Louisiana's story for more than 60 years. It's the five-parish region's own museum — art, history, and culture under one roof, founded back in 1963.

Two exhibits are on now and both run through January 2: "Nellie Lutcher Delightfully Yours," celebrating Lake Charles' own jazz legend, and "Shannon Simmons: World Citizen," a show the museum describes as mysterious, folkloric, and wonderfully strange. It's a good quiet stop between the weekend's louder plans — and a reminder that some of the best things in this town have been here all along.

Practical stuff: the museum is open Tuesday 10 a.m.–2 p.m., Wednesday through Friday 10 a.m.–3 p.m., and Saturday 10 a.m.–2 p.m.

One Good Thing

Bell City High has a band again — for the first time in more than 60 years. Danielle Bourgeois is the school's new director of bands, bringing music back to a campus that hasn't had a program in over six decades. Somewhere in Bell City tonight, kids are learning their first scales on instruments their grandparents never got to play. That's the kind of thing that changes a school.
