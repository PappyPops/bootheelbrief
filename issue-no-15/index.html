The Lead · 160 years, and still feeding its neighbors

Sunday was a big day at Reeves Temple Christian Methodist Episcopal Church in Lake Charles: 160 years. The congregation, founded in 1866 by formerly enslaved people, marked the milestone with a gospel musical and a revival leading into Sunday worship and a community meal. The Rev. Curlee Adams, who came to Reeves Temple from Chicago in August, is the church's newest pastor — and he walked into a history that goes back further than Lake Charles itself has been a city.

That history is the kind you can't get from a plaque. Reeves Chapel ran a school for Black students in 1877. One of its members, Rufus Mayfield Jr., became Lake Charles' first Black city councilman. A hurricane destroyed a church building in 1918; more than a century later, Hurricanes Laura and Delta damaged the property in 2020, and the church reopened in late 2022 after repairs. Through all of it, the congregation kept meeting, kept singing, kept serving.

And it still is. The church runs an outdoor pantry for neighbors who need groceries, and members visit people in nursing homes. Anniversary committee co-chair Sharon Welcome put it plainly: the congregation might not all be blood-related, but they are definitely spiritually related. One hundred and sixty years in, that's the part that hasn't changed. Read the KPLC story →

"A church doesn't last 160 years by accident. It lasts because somebody keeps showing up for the neighbors."

Featured · Light Up the Lake kicks off Christmas on Saturday

Saturday is the big switch-over: Lake Charles kicks off the Christmas season with Light Up the Lake, 1 to 8 p.m. at the Arcade Amphitheater and the lakefront, 1111 Bord du Lac Dr. It's free, and the whole afternoon is stacked — a Christmas Walking Parade at 1, the Lake Charles Community Band at 2, Calcashoo at 3, the Last Minute Big Band at 4:30, and then the official lighting ceremony at 6:15, followed by the lighted boat parade and fireworks over the water at 7:30.

There's also a Holiday Art Walk with more than 30 artisans, Santa's Workshop in Millennium Park, and food trucks. Bring a blanket or a lawn chair — coolers and pets are not permitted. Sports

The Cowboys' season ended the way too many of their games did this year — right at the edge. McNeese fell 19-17 to Lamar on Saturday at Navarre Stadium, watching the Cardinals celebrate a Southland Conference title on the Cowboys' home turf. Freshman quarterback Brady Barrier, making his first start, threw for 164 yards and two touchdowns, and Deagian Camron's 36-yard field goal put McNeese up 17-16 with 1:23 left — before Lamar's Andres Sandoval answered with the winner with 13 seconds to go. Camron's 56-yard try on the final play fell four yards short. The bright spot that will last: senior Omiri Wiggins ran for 125 yards and wrapped up the Southland rushing title. McNeese finishes 5-7, and Lamar takes the league's automatic playoff bid. Full recap →

Business Spotlight · Creative Roots, Lake Charles

If your holiday shopping list still has a few names on it, skip the big-box aisle and walk into Creative Roots at 3204 Ryan St. More than 20 local artisans sell under one roof there — handmade jewelry, pottery, art, and herbal remedies — so the gift you pick up is one nobody else is giving. The shop also runs hands-on workshops if you'd rather make something than buy it. It's the kind of place that makes "shop local" feel less like a slogan and more like a Saturday morning plan.

One Good Thing

Somebody in Iowa made sure 300 families eat well this week. Iowa Mayor Neal Watkins teamed up with Mallett Buildings to put together holiday meals for about 300 families in Iowa, Bell City, Lacassine, and LeBleu Settlement. Families pick up their meals today, 1 to 3 p.m., at Lawrence Toups Jr. Memorial Park. It's the sort of thing that never makes a big headline — a mayor, a local business, and a park full of turkeys — but it's exactly the week for it.
