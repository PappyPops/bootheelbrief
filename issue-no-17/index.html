The Lead · Thanksgiving, the Southwest Louisiana way

There's a particular kind of Thanksgiving morning in Southwest Louisiana. The air is cold enough to see your breath, the sun is already up and working on it, and by mid-morning every kitchen in the parish smells like somebody's grandmother is in charge. The parade's on the TV, the turkey's doing its thing, and the group chat is arguing about what time dinner actually is.

All week, the parish has been feeding each other — churches handing out turkeys, neighbors dropping off groceries, free dinners for anybody who showed up hungry. That's not a program or a press release. It's just what happens here the week of Thanksgiving, and it's the best part of the holiday, better than the food itself.

One thing to watch if you're cooking outside today: the National Weather Service says fire danger is up this afternoon. The air is dry, the wind is gusty, and the drought hasn't let up — so keep the fryer well away from the house, keep the kids back, and don't walk away from it. Then sit down, eat too much pie, and be glad you're here. Thanksgiving week around SWLA →

Sports

McNeese's season ended the way too many of its games did this year — right at the wire. The Cowboys led No. 4 Lamar 17-16 with 1:23 to play Saturday at Navarre Stadium before the Cardinals kicked a field goal with 13 seconds left to win 19-17 and take the Battle of the Border trophy. McNeese finishes 5-7, but running back Omiri Wiggins closed the year with the Southland rushing crown — 933 yards — after 125 yards on Saturday. The Cowboys' basketball team is on the road Saturday at Louisiana, after a 95-69 loss at Texas State on Sunday. And Friday night at 7, the high school quarterfinals: No. 3 Jennings hosts No. 11 Jewel Sumner, No. 2 Lake Charles College Prep hosts No. 7 St. Michael, and No. 12 Welsh travels to No. 4 Mangham — all three SWLA teams two wins from the Superdome. One Good Thing

Two hundred families went home Monday with Thanksgiving covered. Dr. Roishetta Ozane and The Vessel Project spent Monday at the Masonic Lodge on V.E. Washington Street in Lake Charles, serving a free hot meal and handing a turkey or ham and a bag of groceries to the first 200 families who came through — with Queen of the South #14 and Prince Hall #37 Order of the Eastern Star, led by Worthy Matron Monica Levy-Smyre, pitching in. No speeches, no fuss. Just a line of neighbors, a hot plate, and a holiday taken care of. More Thanksgiving giveaways around SWLA →
